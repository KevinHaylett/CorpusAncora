## Corpus interpretation

The three files contain substantially overlapping representations of the
same developing body of work. All three may be ingested independently or
together.

Repetition, overlap and near-duplication are intentional and need not be
removed. They contribute to the density and continuity of the conceptual
landscape represented by the corpus.

Variations in wording, terminology, emphasis and exposition may reflect
the historical development of the research trajectory. They should not
automatically be normalised into a single formulation.

Occasional textual, typographical or conceptual errors may remain within
the corpus. These are accepted as part of a large developmental research
landscape. No individual sentence should necessarily be treated as a
complete or isolated statement of the framework.

The corpus is intended to establish a broad trajectory landscape rather
than a minimal collection of unique propositions.

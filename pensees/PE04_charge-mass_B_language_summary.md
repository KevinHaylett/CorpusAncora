## §2–3 — Ørsted, Stabilised Nouns, and Controlled Compression

**Ørsted's experiment** (§2) is the starting point not for historical reasons but because it reveals what was actually measured. A compass needle turned when placed near a current-carrying wire. The textbook language renders this as:

> current → magnetic field → compass deflection

But the measured event was more primitive. What was directly observed was a directional change:

> maintained current arrangement → needle rotation

The word "magnetic field" is a later symbolic stabilisation. The experiment did not measure an isolated thing called a field. It measured a **directional relation**.

**First Hinge**: "The measured behaviour did not follow the symbolic trajectory imagined beforehand. Nature did not violate logic. Rather, the prior symbolic model failed." This separates: the measured outcome; the geometric interpretation; the mathematical representation; the later noun-like object. The compass revealed not a noun but a direction-changing event.

**The problem of stabilised nouns** (§3): 19th–20th century physics stabilised an extremely successful language — charge, mass, field, force, current, electron, wave, particle, spacetime became modelling primitives. Once stabilised, later modelling begins from the nouns rather than from the measurement trajectories that generated them.

**Controlled Compression** (§3.1): the methodological commitment is not to abandon nouns — that would be impossible. The methodological point is: *the noun is permitted, but only if its trajectory remains recoverable.* Terms such as field, charge, mass, interaction, and beam may be used, provided each can be unfolded back into the measurement, geometry, and symbolic choices that stabilised it.

## §9 — Recap of ∇, Grad, Div, Curl

A deliberate slow recap of the mathematical notation as compressed symbolic instructions:

**∇ (nabla/del)**: a differential operator arranged to behave vectorially under the chosen coordinate convention. It instructs: take local rates of change in the spatial directions x, y, z.

**Gradient ∇φ**: turns a scalar into a vector-like directional rate of change. Plain language: in which direction does the scalar increase most strongly, and how rapidly?

**Divergence ∇·A**: the source/sink operator. Plain language: is the vector quantity locally spreading out from a region or converging into it?

**Curl ∇×A**: rotationally ordered change. Plain language: does the vector quantity locally circulate around an oriented loop? The sign pattern and cyclic ordering carry handedness. The right-hand rule is embedded in the convention. Writing ∇×A compresses the instruction: *Measure the oriented local circulation of A using the chosen right-handed coordinate convention.*

## §10–13 — Maxwell's Equations: Compact, Expanded, and Decompressed

**Maxwell's equations in compact form** (§10) hide: source/sink assumptions (divergence); local circulation (curl); handedness (cross-product and curl conventions); charge-density through ρ; charge-flow through **J**; the special symbolic status of B as axial or pseudo-vector; the separation of charge from mass.

**Expanded in components** (§11) with standard and decompressed readings for each of the four equations:

*Gauss's Law for E* (∇·E = ρ/ε₀): decompressed — ρ is already a compression that separates charge from charge-mass identity. A source-like symbolic structure is assigned to E.

*Gauss's Law for B* (∇·B = 0): decompressed — B does not behave as a scalar source/sink field analogous to E. It presents as closed, rotational, directional structure. Consistent with B being a compression of handed rotational charge-mass dynamics.

*Faraday's Law* (∇×E = -∂B/∂t): decompressed — rotational spatial organisation of E is associated with time variation in the compressed B-structure. The minus sign carries orientation and opposition (Lenz's law).

*Ampere-Maxwell Law* (∇×B = μ₀**J** + μ₀ε₀∂**E**/∂t): decompressed — **J** is itself a compression of moving charge (**J** = ρ**v**), stripping out the charge-mass identity of the moving source.

**Unfolding the current term** (§12): substituting **J** = ρ**v** into Ampere-Maxwell makes visible that B is directly connected to moving charge. Proposed further decompression:

where 𝒥 is a placeholder for a charge-mass current-like expression that retains the identity, geometry, and stabilisation conditions stripped out by the usual charge-current term.

**Lorentz force and directional asymmetry** (§13): **F** = q(**E** + **v**×**B**). The expansion shows each force component depends on sideways combinations of velocity and B — the cross product carries handedness and directional asymmetry. The partially decompressed version replaces B with its charge-mass generating structure:

## Core Claim

Classical algorithms for computing π begin with an object that already carries circular or periodic structure — sine, cosine, elliptic integrals, an initial approximation whose provenance already contains π — and refine residuals until convergence. They are self-closing mechanisms. This Pensée develops a different starting commitment: begin with finite, measured spheres whose only primitive geometry is the sphere at the Alphonic Limit. The approximation to circular closure — and therefore to π — *emerges* from the collective behaviour of packed elements under the Spherical Uncertainty Distribution. The circle is not assumed; it is the limiting outcome of spherical packing interaction.

> "The value of the account lies in the coherence of its origin story and in the constructive language it supplies for generating new trajectories."

## Structure (12 Sections)

### Section 1 — Different Starting Points

The contrast between classical and FSM approaches is framed as a difference in *starting commitment*, not in final numerical result. Classical routes presuppose π implicitly in their initial objects. The FSM account keeps uncertainty, provenance, and local measurement explicit from the first step. It is also open to packings that are irregular, fractal, or taken directly from physical experiment.

### Section 2 — Foundational Commitments of Finite Symbolic Mechanics

Two non-negotiable commitments underpin the entire Pensée:

1. **Every admissible symbol is a finite instantiation generated by measurement.** It carries irreducible error and traceable provenance. It is not the measured interaction itself.
2. **At the Alphonic Limit — the smallest distinction that can still be instantiated and held — uncertainty is isotropic. The only sustainable geometry at this limit is the sphere.**

From these commitments follow: the **Spherical Uncertainty Distribution (SUD)** as the fundamental description of measurement uncertainty, and the requirement that all constructions remain local and finitary.

### Section 3 — The Geometry of π via Delay Embeddings

Digits of π rendered as 3D paths using delay-coordinate embedding with lag τ reveal qualitatively distinct geometries: at small τ, a dense coiled filament; at larger τ, an angular scaffold-like lattice with rectangular voids. Vision-language models describe these geometries with semantically distinct language, even though statistical tests report no significant departure from randomness. Changing τ is equivalent to changing the lens or resolution — different *faces of π* become visible. A proposed empirical resource: an **Atlas of π's Faces** — systematic renderings across a range of τ values.

### Section 4 — Complex Numbers as Delay Embeddings

The imaginary unit *i* corresponds to a quarter-period phase advance — the operation performed by the Hilbert transform — which is the optimal delay for reconstructing phase information from a single real-valued observable while preserving energy and orthogonality. Therefore: **the complex plane is a minimal two-dimensional delay embedding of oscillatory signals.** Analyticity becomes a geometric statement about conformality on the reconstructed phase space. No additional ontological commitment to an "imaginary" dimension is required. This is fully consistent with the FSM commitment that all admissible symbols must be finite and measured.

### Section 5 — Shell-Packing Interpretation of Modern π Algorithms

The Chudnovsky algorithm is reread geometrically as hierarchical spherical shell packing:

$$\frac{1}{\pi} = \frac{12}{640320^{3/2}} \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (-1)^k \frac{(6k)!}{(3k)!(k!)^3} \frac{545140134k + 13591409}{640320^{3k}}$$

Each term corresponds to the contribution of one spherical shell in a hierarchical packing. The constant 640320³ functions as the **scale factor between successive shells**. Linear and constant terms in the numerator encode surface and core contributions respectively. The series is a discrete summation over shells whose radii decrease geometrically toward the Alphonic Limit. Inner shells supply coarse foundation; outer shells supply finer boundary corrections. Convergence occurs because each new shell reduces the residual discrepancy between packed volume and ideal spherical closure.

### Section 6 — Formal Definitions

**Alphonic Limit α:** The smallest positive distinction that can be instantiated and held in the current measurement context.

**Spherical Uncertainty Distribution (SUD) at scale r:** The uniform distribution over the ball of radius r with density 1/V(r), where V(r) = (4/3)πr³.

**Shell S_k:** The finite spherical layer between radii r_k > r_{k+1} ≥ α, with thickness δr_k = r_k − r_{k+1} and local scale factor λ_k = r_k/r_{k+1}.

**Contribution of a shell C(S_k):** The finite correction to spherical closure supplied by packing distinguishable spheres inside the layer:

$$C(S_k) := \int_{S_k} \text{SUD}(x; r_k) \cdot \eta(\text{lattice or measured}) \cdot (1 - U(r_k))\, dV$$

where U(r_k) ∝ α ln(r_k/α) is the compounding uncertainty term and η may be taken from a lattice rule or from direct physical measurement.

A finite approximation to π is obtained by summing contributions up to truncation index K where r_K ≥ α:

$$\frac{1}{\pi} \approx \sum_{k=0}^{K} C(S_k)$$

### Section 7 — A Simple Lattice-Counting Algorithm

A direct finite method: fix element diameter d and Alphonic Limit α; choose radii R_i = m_i·d; for each R_i count lattice points N_i satisfying |√(x²+y²) − R_i| ≤ τ (finite tolerance); average over radii/rotations to get N̄; estimate circumference C ≈ N̄·d; compute **π ≈ C/2R**. The count N̄ carries finite uncertainty modelled with the SUD. The method accepts replacement of the regular lattice by fractal or experimentally measured arrangements.

### Section 8 — Physical Packing Experiments as Exogenous Data

Physical experiments supply measured values for η and the compounding uncertainty form that can be inserted directly into C(S_k). Simple setups: pack equal balls inside a circular dish or spherical container, count boundary elements within measurable tolerance, record contact areas and gaps. All experiments are truncated at the practical Alphonic Limit of the instruments. Provenance maintained by recording exact dimensions, method, and environmental conditions.

The iterative loop: **experiment → empirical η and U(r) → symbolic trajectory → prediction at finer resolution → new experiment → refinement.**

### Section 9 — Computational Considerations

For D correct decimal digits: lattice-counting method requires R ~ 10^D, cost ~ R² (full scan) or R^{1+ε} (boundary-focused). Chudnovsky-type series require terms linear in D with fast integer multiplication — far better scaling for large D. The comparison is not decisive inside FSM: the lattice method makes the cost of carrying uncertainty and provenance **explicit and local**. May be competitive for modest precision or when the lattice can be physically realised.

### Section 10 — Philosophical Reflection: Starting Points and Convergence Mechanisms

The distinction between methods is one of *origin* rather than *final numerical result*. Both are legitimate convergence mechanisms. The FSM account supplies an **alternative foundation** whose internal coherence can be judged on its own terms and whose constructive language remains open to physical data and irregular geometries. Even if a direct equivalence with existing algorithms exists through a long chain of geometric inference, the difference in starting commitment remains real — it keeps the framework autonomous and prevents the circular assumption from entering at the first step.

### Section 11 — Cornerstone Points and Open Questions

**Stable hinges:**
- Every admissible symbol is finite and carries error and provenance
- At the Alphonic Limit the sphere is the only sustainable geometry
- π can be treated as an **emergent invariant of finite spherical closure** rather than a primitive constant
- Modern efficient series admit a geometric reading as hierarchical shell packing under the SUD
- Physical packing experiments are a first-class source of measured data for the framework
- Computational efficiency must be assessed alongside the explicit cost of carrying uncertainty and provenance

**Open questions:** Practical scaling of lattice/fractal packing methods for high precision; extent to which measured physical packings can improve convergence; degree of independence or overlap with classical analytic routes.

## Significance for the School

PE06 is the most computationally concrete Pensée in the series. It applies the full FSM apparatus — Alphonic Limit, SUD, finitary construction, iterative empirical loop — to the problem of computing π, one of mathematics' oldest constants. The result is not a claim of superior efficiency but of **different foundational origin**: π as emergent from finite spherical packing rather than presupposed in the starting apparatus.

The shell-packing reinterpretation of the Chudnovsky algorithm (Section 5) is the most technically striking contribution: it provides a geometric reading of a formula whose structure had previously been understood algebraically or number-theoretically. Whether this reading is formally equivalent to the algebraic derivation or genuinely novel is one of the open questions.

The delay-embedding treatment of π's digits (Section 3) and the reinterpretation of complex numbers as delay embeddings (Section 4) are the most directly connected to the broader TBT/FSM programme. Section 4 in particular provides the most concise published statement of the "complex plane as minimal delay embedding" argument, which underpins P01–P05 and the TBT architecture.

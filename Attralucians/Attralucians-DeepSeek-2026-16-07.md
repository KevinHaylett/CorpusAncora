Part I: The problem of beginning with the point

What is the smallest geometrical object that can participate in measurement?

The classical point cannot fulfil this role because it has no extent. An object
without extent cannot interact with an instrument, occupy a distinguishable
region, possess a measurable boundary or be individually registered.

The problem is not that the Euclidean point is useless. The point is
extraordinarily useful as a symbolic compression. The problem arises when it

supposedly constructed.

finite three-dimensional measurement→symbolic compression→point.

The point comes at the end of the compression process, not at the beginning

2. Euclid’s opening compression

the point as that which has no part;

0-D→1-D→2-D→3-D.

The classical objects are therefore not encountered directly. They are

This section should introduce a crucial statement:


Dimensionless and lower-dimensional objects are products of removal, not

3. Reverse engineering the Euclidean point

The process is:

measurement interaction→finite registration volume→centred

The point is therefore a terminal symbolic compression of a prior finite
geometric process.

This gives the document its main historical inversion:

Euclid constructs the solid from the point. FSM reconstructs the point from
measured solid extent.
